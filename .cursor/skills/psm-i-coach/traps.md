# Coach checklist (2020 Scrum Guide)

Fallback and item-writing checklist. The fetched Guide wins if this file disagrees with it. Do not treat this file as a second source of truth.

## Entity catalog for flash cards

Theory: Empiricism, Lean thinking, Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation.
Values: Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage.
Team: Scrum Team, Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers, Cross-functional, Self-managing.
Events: Sprint, Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, Product Backlog refinement.
Artifacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment.
Commitments: Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done.
Related: Product, Stakeholder, Impediment.

Refinement is an ongoing activity, not a sixth event. Do not flash it as an event.

## Relationships that decide exam items

- Empiricism: knowledge comes from experience and from decisions based on what is observed. Lean thinking reduces waste and focuses on the essentials.
- Transparency enables inspection. Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful. Inspection without adaptation is pointless.
- Adaptation is harder when people are not empowered or self-managing. The team adapts the moment it learns something through inspection.
- Five events give cadence for inspection. Four of them are opportunities to inspect and adapt inside the containing event, the Sprint.
- Each event is a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt. Skipping one loses a chance to inspect and adapt.
- Values: Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage. Embodied values bring the pillars to life and build trust.
- Scrum Team = one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. No sub-teams or hierarchies. Cross-functional and self-managing (who does what, when, and how). Typically 10 or fewer. Cohesive on one objective at a time: the Product Goal.
- Several Scrum Teams on one product share the same Product Goal, Product Backlog, and Product Owner.
- The whole Scrum Team is accountable for a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.
- Product Owner is accountable for maximizing value and for effective Product Backlog management: Product Goal, creating and communicating items, ordering items, transparency of the backlog. May delegate the work; remains accountable. One person, not a committee. Others change the backlog by convincing the Product Owner.
- Developers are accountable for the Sprint Backlog plan, adhering to the Definition of Done, adapting the plan daily toward the Sprint Goal, and holding each other accountable. They select Sprint items through discussion with the Product Owner. They size the work. No one else tells them how to turn items into Increments.
- Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide, and for the team's effectiveness. A true leader who serves the team, the Product Owner, and the organization. Causes removal of impediments. Ensures events happen and stay inside the timebox. Coaches self-management and cross-functionality. Helps the Product Owner with Goal definition, backlog techniques, empirical planning, and stakeholder collaboration as requested. Does not become the team's task assigner or backlog owner.
- Sprint: fixed length, one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately. No changes that endanger the Sprint Goal. Quality does not decrease. The Product Backlog is refined as needed. Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned.
- Only the Product Owner may cancel a Sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.
- Forecasting charts (burn-downs, burn-ups, cumulative flow) may be used. They do not replace empiricism.
- Sprint Planning is for the whole Scrum Team and creates the Sprint plan. Topics: Why (Sprint Goal; Product Owner proposes how value could increase; the team defines the goal; the goal is finalized before Planning ends), What (Developers select items), How (Developers plan the work). Timebox: at most 8 hours for a one-month Sprint; usually shorter when the Sprint is shorter. Others may be invited for advice.
- Sprint Backlog = Sprint Goal (why) + selected Product Backlog items (what) + plan (how). A plan by and for the Developers. Updated throughout the Sprint. Inspected at the Daily Scrum.
- Sprint Goal: single objective of the Sprint; commitment of the Sprint Backlog; commitment by the Developers; flexibility in the exact work. If work differs from expectations, Developers and the Product Owner negotiate scope without affecting the Sprint Goal.
- Daily Scrum: 15 minutes, for the Developers, same time and place, inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog. Developers choose structure and techniques. Product Owner or Scrum Master joins as a Developer only when actively working Sprint Backlog items. Not the only time the plan may change. Creates focus and self-management. Not prescribed as three questions.
- Sprint Review: inspect the Sprint outcome and decide future adaptations. Team and key stakeholders. Progress toward the Product Goal. Working session, not only a presentation. Product Backlog may be adjusted. Second to last event. At most 4 hours for a one-month Sprint. Not a release gate.
- Sprint Retrospective: plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Inspect people, interactions, process, tools, and the Definition of Done. Most impactful improvements as soon as possible; may go on the next Sprint Backlog. Concludes the Sprint. At most 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.
- Commitments: Product Backlog → Product Goal; Sprint Backlog → Sprint Goal; Increment → Definition of Done. They reinforce empiricism and the values.
- Product Backlog: emergent, ordered, single source of work for the Scrum Team. Product Goal is in the Product Backlog. The rest emerges to define what fulfills that goal. One Product Goal at a time: fulfill or abandon it before the next.
- Product: a vehicle to deliver value. Clear boundary, known stakeholders, well-defined users or customers. Service, physical product, or more abstract.
- Refinement: ongoing. Adds detail such as description, order, and size. Items that can be Done in one Sprint are ready for selection. No Definition of Ready in the Guide.
- Increment: concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Additive and thoroughly verified. Must be usable. Several Increments may be created in a Sprint. May be delivered before the Sprint ends. Work that does not meet the Definition of Done is not part of an Increment, cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review, and returns to the Product Backlog.
- Definition of Done: formal description of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product. The moment an item meets it, an Increment is born. If the organization has a standard, every Scrum Team follows it as a minimum. Otherwise the Scrum Team creates one appropriate for the product. Developers conform to it. Multiple teams on one product mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done.
- Scrum is purposefully incomplete and immutable. Using only parts is not Scrum. Complementary tactics are described elsewhere.

## Distinctions to force into drills

- Product Goal (product's future state, long-term, in the Product Backlog) vs Sprint Goal (why this Sprint is valuable, in the Sprint Backlog) vs Definition of Done (quality bar of the Increment).
- Sprint Review (product, stakeholders, Product Goal, what next) vs Retrospective (team effectiveness, Definition of Done, how we work) vs Daily Scrum (Developers, Sprint Goal, today's plan).
- Accountable vs the person who may do the work (Product Owner may delegate backlog work and remains accountable).
- Sprint Goal as the commitment vs a forecast of selected items. Scope can move; the goal and the quality bar do not get traded away.
- Self-managing (2020) vs self-organizing (older wording). Teach the 2020 term.
- Accountabilities vs "roles."
- "True leaders who serve" vs the job title "servant-leader."
- Scrum Master ensures the Daily Scrum happens vs Developers own its structure.
- Causing removal of impediments vs personally fixing every impediment.
- Ordering (Product Owner accountability) vs sizing (Developers) vs selecting Sprint items (Developers, in discussion with the Product Owner).
- Five events vs four events inside the Sprint.
- Ready for selection (transparent enough to be Done in one Sprint) vs a Definition of Ready gate.

## Not required by the Guide

Classify as outside the Guide, or allowed only as a local technique: user stories, story points, planning poker, velocity targets, Sprint 0, hardening Sprint, release Sprint, Definition of Ready, the three Daily Scrum questions, a Product Owner committee or proxy, a project-manager accountability, separate tester or architect accountabilities, Scrum of Scrums, SAFe, Nexus, mandatory burn-down, assigning tasks to Developers, the Scrum Master as meeting chair of every event, the Sprint Review as sign-off, holding back a Done Increment until the Review, cutting the Definition of Done mid-Sprint, more than one Product Owner or Product Backlog for one product.

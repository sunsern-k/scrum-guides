# Entities

Canonical definitions from the November 2020 Scrum Guide. Short lines marked Guide are quotations. Other lines are paraphrase. `Topics` lists the serving copies that must be edited when the entry changes.

## Scrum

- Topics: 09
- Sense: A lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. Purposefully incomplete. Immutable: implementing only parts of it is possible, and the result is not Scrum.
- Guide: "Scrum exists only in its entirety."
- Not this: a method that requires user stories, velocity, or extra events.

## Empiricism

- Topics: 01
- Sense: Knowledge comes from experience and from making decisions based on what is observed. Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking.
- Not this: deciding from a prediction of work that has not happened.

## Lean thinking

- Topics: 01
- Sense: Reduces waste and focuses on the essentials. Paired with empiricism as a foundation of Scrum. The Guide does not define a separate lean practice set.
- Not this: a requirement to adopt a particular lean toolkit.

## Transparency

- Topics: 01
- Sense: The emergent process and work must be visible to those performing the work and those receiving it. Important decisions are based on the perceived state of the three artifacts. Low transparency leads to decisions that diminish value and increase risk.
- Guide: "Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful."
- Not this: a status report that replaces the artifacts.

## Inspection

- Topics: 01
- Sense: Artifacts and progress toward agreed goals are inspected frequently and diligently to detect undesirable variances. The five events provide cadence.
- Guide: "Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is considered pointless."
- Not this: inspection that never changes the plan or the product.

## Adaptation

- Topics: 01, 07
- Sense: When any aspect of the process deviates outside acceptable limits, or the product is unacceptable, the process or the materials must be adjusted as soon as possible. Harder when people are not empowered or self-managing.
- Guide: "A Scrum Team is expected to adapt the moment it learns anything new through inspection."
- Not this: waiting for the next quarterly plan to change course.

## Commitment

- Topics: 02, 05
- Sense: One of the five Scrum values. The Scrum Team commits to achieving its goals and to supporting each other. Also the name of the element each artifact contains (Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done). Those commitments are not a promise to finish every selected item.
- Not this: a guarantee of scope.

## Focus

- Topics: 02
- Sense: The Scrum Team's primary focus is the work of the Sprint, to make the best possible progress toward its goals.
- Not this: a rule that the team may only discuss one Product Backlog item.

## Openness

- Topics: 02
- Sense: The Scrum Team and its stakeholders are open about the work and the challenges.
- Not this: a requirement to publish a particular metric.

## Respect

- Topics: 02
- Sense: Scrum Team members respect each other to be capable, independent people, and are respected as such by the people with whom they work.
- Not this: a Scrum Master who assigns work because the Developers are not trusted to plan.

## Courage

- Topics: 02
- Sense: Scrum Team members have the courage to do the right thing and to work on tough problems.
- Not this: a license to ignore the Definition of Done or the Sprint Goal.

## Scrum Team

- Topics: 03
- Sense: The fundamental unit of Scrum. One Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. No sub-teams or hierarchies. Cross-functional and self-managing. Typically 10 or fewer. Focused on one objective at a time, the Product Goal. Accountable as a whole for a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.
- Not this: a team split into a coding team and a testing team, or a team with a project-manager accountability.

## Product Owner

- Topics: 03, 10, 11, 12
- Sense: Accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team, and for effective Product Backlog management. One person, not a committee. May represent many stakeholders. May delegate the work and remains accountable.
- Guide: "Those wanting to change the Product Backlog can do so by trying to convince the Product Owner."
- Not this: a committee, a proxy, or the person who tells Developers how to build the Increment.

## Scrum Master

- Topics: 03, 08, 09
- Sense: Accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, and for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. A true leader who serves the Scrum Team, the Product Owner, and the organization.
- Guide: "Scrum Masters are true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organization."
- Not this: the 2017 job title "servant-leader," a task assigner, or the owner of the Product Backlog.

## Developers

- Topics: 03, 04, 06, 07, 11
- Sense: The people in the Scrum Team committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint. Skills vary by domain. Accountable for the Sprint plan, adhering to the Definition of Done, adapting that plan each day toward the Sprint Goal, and holding each other accountable as professionals.
- Not this: only programmers, or people who take tasks assigned by the Scrum Master.

## Cross-functional

- Topics: 03, 09
- Sense: The Scrum Team members have all the skills necessary to create value each Sprint. They share or acquire skills as needed.
- Not this: a separate testing or analysis team outside the Scrum Team.

## Self-managing

- Topics: 03, 07, 09
- Sense: The Scrum Team internally decides who does what, when, and how.
- Guide: "They are also self-managing, meaning they internally decide who does what, when, and how."
- Not this: the older word "self-organizing" as the 2020 term, or a team whose Sprint tasks are assigned from outside.

## Sprint

- Topics: 04
- Sense: The heartbeat of Scrum. A fixed-length container event of one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one concludes. All other events happen inside it. Creates at least a monthly inspection of progress toward the Product Goal.
- Not this: a phase that can be lengthened when the forecast slips, or a Sprint 0.

## Sprint Planning

- Topics: 04
- Sense: Initiates the Sprint by laying out the work. The whole Scrum Team collaborates. Topics are Why, What, and How. Timeboxed to a maximum of eight hours for a one-month Sprint. Usually shorter for a shorter Sprint.
- Not this: a meeting owned by the Scrum Master in which Developers are told what to pull.

## Daily Scrum

- Topics: 04, 08
- Sense: 15 minutes, for the Developers, same time and place every working day. Inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapts the Sprint Backlog. Developers choose the structure and techniques.
- Not this: a status meeting for the Product Owner, or a required three-question format.

## Sprint Review

- Topics: 04, 12
- Sense: Inspects the outcome of the Sprint and determines future adaptations. The Scrum Team and key stakeholders discuss progress toward the Product Goal. A working session. Timeboxed to a maximum of four hours for a one-month Sprint. Second to last event of the Sprint.
- Guide: "The Sprint Review should never be considered a gate to releasing value."
- Not this: a sign-off demo, or the only moment an Increment may be delivered.

## Sprint Retrospective

- Topics: 04, 06
- Sense: Plans ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Inspects individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done. Concludes the Sprint. Timeboxed to a maximum of three hours for a one-month Sprint.
- Not this: an optional event, or a second Sprint Review about the product increment.

## Product Backlog refinement

- Topics: 04, 11
- Sense: The ongoing activity of breaking down and further defining Product Backlog items into smaller, more precise items. Adds detail such as description, order, and size. Not a Scrum event.
- Not this: a sixth event, or a Definition of Ready gate.

## Product Backlog

- Topics: 05, 11
- Sense: An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product. The single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team. Contains the Product Goal. Commitment: the Product Goal.
- Not this: one of several backlogs for the same product, or a list the stakeholders reorder directly.

## Sprint Backlog

- Topics: 05, 07
- Sense: The Sprint Goal (why), the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint (what), and an actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how). A plan by and for the Developers. Updated throughout the Sprint. Commitment: the Sprint Goal.
- Not this: a fixed scope contract, or a plan the Product Owner edits alone.

## Increment

- Topics: 05, 06, 10
- Sense: A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Additive to all prior Increments and thoroughly verified. Must be usable. Multiple Increments may be created in a Sprint. Commitment: the Definition of Done.
- Guide: "Work cannot be considered part of an Increment unless it meets the Definition of Done."
- Not this: unfinished work shown at the Sprint Review, or a release that waits for the Sprint Review.

## Product Goal

- Topics: 05, 10
- Sense: A future state of the product that the Scrum Team can plan against. It lives in the Product Backlog. The long-term objective. The team must fulfill or abandon one objective before taking on the next. Commitment of the Product Backlog.
- Not this: another name for the Sprint Goal, or several Product Goals pursued at once.

## Sprint Goal

- Topics: 04, 05
- Sense: The single objective for the Sprint. Created in Sprint Planning and finalized before that event ends. Commitment of the Sprint Backlog, by the Developers. Creates coherence and focus, and allows flexibility in the exact work.
- Not this: a promise to complete every selected Product Backlog item.

## Definition of Done

- Topics: 05, 06
- Sense: A formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product. The moment a Product Backlog item meets it, an Increment is born. Creates a shared understanding of what "done" means. Commitment of the Increment.
- Not this: a per-Sprint quality bargain, or a Definition of Ready.

## Product

- Topics: 10, 12
- Sense: A vehicle to deliver value. It has a clear boundary, known stakeholders, and well-defined users or customers. It could be a service, a physical product, or something more abstract.
- Guide: "A product is a vehicle to deliver value."
- Not this: a project, or a component team with no users.

## Stakeholder

- Topics: 12
- Sense: People with a stake in the product, including those receiving the work. Not a fourth accountability. Key stakeholders collaborate at the Sprint Review. They change the Product Backlog by convincing the Product Owner.
- Not this: a committee that orders the Product Backlog, or a required daily attendee.

## Impediment

- Topics: 08, 09
- Sense: Something blocking the Scrum Team's progress. The Scrum Master causes its removal. The Guide does not make the Scrum Master the only person who may remove it, and it does not define impediment as an artifact.
- Not this: a rule that the Scrum Master personally fixes every blocker before the Developers may act.

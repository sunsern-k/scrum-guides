Revisit the key topics

Twelve topics from the November 2020 Scrum Guide, in study order. Read one, then use Next. Relationship diagrams sit above the sentences. Underlined words are entities and open their definitions.

Topic 1 of 12

Empiricism

Guide sense

Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking. Empiricism asserts that knowledge comes from experience and from making decisions based on what is observed. Lean thinking reduces waste and focuses on the essentials. The three pillars are transparency, inspection, and adaptation. Scrum's events work because they implement those pillars.

Entities

  • Empiricism — decisions come from what is observed, not from a prediction of work that has not happened.
  • Lean thinking — reduce waste and focus on the essentials. The Guide names it and does not prescribe a lean toolkit.
  • Transparency — the process and the work are visible to the people doing the work and the people receiving it. Decisions rest on the perceived state of the three artifacts.
  • Inspection — artifacts and progress toward agreed goals are inspected frequently and diligently. The five events provide the cadence.
  • Adaptation — adjust the process or the product as soon as it is outside acceptable limits.

Relationships

  • R01. Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.
  • R02. Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is pointless.
  • R03. Adaptation is harder when people are not empowered or self-managing.
  • R04. The Scrum Team adapts the moment it learns anything new through inspection.
  • R05. The five events are the cadence for inspection.
  • R08. Burn-downs, burn-ups, and cumulative flow may be used. They do not replace empiricism. In complex work, only what has already happened supports a forward-looking decision.

Exam trap

A chart of remaining work looks empirical and is still only a forecast. The Guide says these practices do not replace empiricism.

Not this

Empiricism is not a phase-gate plan, and it is not a requirement to install a particular lean method.

Topic 2 of 12

Scrum Values

Guide sense

Successful use of Scrum depends on people becoming more proficient in living five values: Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage. The decisions, the steps, and the way Scrum is used should reinforce these values, not diminish them. The team learns the values as it works with the events and artifacts.

Entities

  • Commitment — the Scrum Team commits to achieving its goals and to supporting each other.
  • Focus — the primary focus is the work of the Sprint, so the team can make the best possible progress toward its goals.
  • Openness — the Scrum Team and its stakeholders are open about the work and the challenges.
  • Respect — members respect each other as capable, independent people, and are respected as such by the people they work with.
  • Courage — members have the courage to do the right thing and to work on tough problems.

Relationships

  • R07. When these values are embodied by the Scrum Team and the people they work with, the pillars of transparency, inspection, and adaptation come to life, building trust.
  • The value of commitment and the Sprint Goal are related and not the same claim. The value is commitment to goals and to each other. The Sprint commitment is the Sprint Goal, with flexibility in the exact work.

Exam trap

"Commitment" is often taught as a promise to finish every Product Backlog item selected for the Sprint. The Guide commits the team to its goals, and the Sprint's commitment is the Sprint Goal.

Not this

The values are not a sixth event, and they do not add a report, a metric, or a second accountability.

Topic 3 of 12

Scrum Team

Guide sense

The fundamental unit of Scrum is a Scrum Team: one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. There are no sub-teams or hierarchies. The team is cross-functional and self-managing, typically 10 or fewer people, and focused on one objective at a time, the Product Goal. The whole Scrum Team is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.

Entities

  • Scrum Team — one cohesive unit. Accountable together for the Increment. Empowered by the organization to manage its own work.
  • Developers — committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint. Accountable for the Sprint plan, the Definition of Done, daily adaptation toward the Sprint Goal, and holding each other accountable.
  • Product Owner — accountable for maximizing product value and for effective Product Backlog management. One person, not a committee.
  • Scrum Master — accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide and for the team's effectiveness. A true leader who serves.
  • Cross-functional — the members have all the skills necessary to create value each Sprint.
  • Self-managing — they internally decide who does what, when, and how. The 2020 word is self-managing.

Relationships

  • R09. The team consists of one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. Scrum defines no fourth accountability.
  • R10. No sub-teams or hierarchies inside the Scrum Team.
  • R11. Cross-functional and self-managing are properties of the Scrum Team, not optional add-ons.
  • R12. Typically 10 or fewer. If the team is too large, reorganize into multiple cohesive Scrum Teams on the same product.
  • R13. Those teams share the same Product Goal, Product Backlog, and Product Owner.
  • R14. The entire Scrum Team is accountable for a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.
  • R15 and R16. Value and Product Backlog management stay with the Product Owner. The deeper rules are topics 10 and 11. Developer planning and the Scrum Master's services are topics 7, 8, and 9.

Exam trap

Calling the three positions "roles," or splitting Developers into a development team and a test team, recreates a hierarchy the Guide removed. The 2020 term is accountabilities, inside one Scrum Team.

Not this

There is no project-manager accountability, no component team required by Scrum, and no separate Development Team accountability. "Development Team" is older wording.

Topic 4 of 12

Events

Guide sense

The Sprint is a container for all other events. Each event is a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt. Together they create regularity and minimize the need for meetings Scrum does not define. Optimally, events are held at the same time and place. For a shorter Sprint, the longer events are usually shorter. The Daily Scrum stays 15 minutes.

Entities

  • Sprint — fixed length, one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately. During it: no change that endangers the Sprint Goal; quality does not decrease; the Product Backlog is refined as needed; scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned.
  • Sprint Planning — whole Scrum Team. Why, What, How. At most 8 hours for a one-month Sprint.
  • Daily Scrum — 15 minutes for the Developers. Inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog.
  • Sprint Review — inspect the Sprint outcome and decide future adaptations with key stakeholders. At most 4 hours for a one-month Sprint. Second to last event.
  • Sprint Retrospective — increase quality and effectiveness. Concludes the Sprint. At most 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.
  • Product Backlog refinement — an ongoing activity, not an event.

Relationships

  • R05 and R06. Five events provide inspection cadence. Four of them are inspect-and-adapt events inside the Sprint.
  • R32. The Sprint contains Planning, the Daily Scrum, the Review, and the Retrospective. Refinement is not a fifth inner event.
  • R33. Length is one month or less, and the next Sprint starts immediately.
  • R34. Scope can move through negotiation with the Product Owner. The Sprint Goal and the quality bar do not.
  • R35. Only the Product Owner may cancel a Sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.
  • R36 and R37. Why: the Product Owner proposes how the product could increase its value; the team defines the Sprint Goal; the goal is finalized before Planning ends. What: the Developers select items. How: the Developers plan the work, at their sole discretion.
  • R38. Planning timebox: maximum eight hours for a one-month Sprint. Others may be invited for advice.
  • R39 and R40. The Daily Scrum is 15 minutes, same time and place, for the Developers.
  • R41. The Product Owner or Scrum Master participates as a Developer only when actively working on Sprint Backlog items.
  • R42 and R43. Developers choose the structure. They may replan throughout the day, not only at the Daily Scrum.
  • R44 and R46. The Review is a working session with key stakeholders about progress toward the Product Goal. The Product Backlog may be adjusted. It is not only a presentation.
  • R45. An Increment may be delivered before the end of the Sprint. The Review is not a release gate.
  • R47, R48, and R49. The Retrospective inspects people, interactions, process, tools, and the Definition of Done. The most impactful improvements happen as soon as possible and may go on the next Sprint Backlog. The Retrospective closes the Sprint.
  • R50. Skipping a prescribed event loses an opportunity to inspect and adapt.
  • R63. Refinement adds description, order, and size. It is not an event.

Exam trap

The three Daily Scrum questions (yesterday, today, impediments) are a common format. The Guide does not require them. Developers may use any structure that focuses on the Sprint Goal and yields a plan for the next day.

Not this

Sprint 0, a hardening Sprint, and a release Sprint are not events in the Guide. The Sprint is not extended to protect a forecast.

Topic 5 of 12

Artifacts

Guide sense

Scrum's artifacts represent work or value and are designed to maximize transparency. Each artifact contains a commitment so progress can be measured against something that enhances transparency and focus. The commitments reinforce empiricism and the Scrum values.

Entities

  • Product Backlog — emergent, ordered, the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.
  • Product Goal — the commitment of the Product Backlog. A future state of the product, in the Product Backlog. The long-term objective.
  • Sprint Backlog — Sprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items, and the plan for delivering the Increment.
  • Sprint Goal — the commitment of the Sprint Backlog. The single objective of the Sprint.
  • Increment — a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Additive, thoroughly verified, and usable.
  • Definition of Done — the commitment of the Increment. The quality measures required for the product. Topic 6 holds the rules.
  • Commitment — the element each artifact contains. It is not a promise to deliver every selected item.

Relationships

  • R51. Product Backlog → Product Goal. The rest of the Product Backlog emerges to define what will fulfill that goal.
  • R52. Sprint Backlog → Sprint Goal. The Sprint Goal, the selected items, and the plan are together the Sprint Backlog.
  • R53. Increment → Definition of Done. These three commitments reinforce empiricism and the values.
  • R54. The Product Backlog is the single source of work for the Scrum Team.
  • R55. The team fulfills or abandons one Product Goal before taking on the next.
  • R56. The Sprint Backlog is a plan by and for the Developers. They update it through the Sprint.
  • R57. The Sprint Goal is a commitment by the Developers and still leaves flexibility in the exact work. If the work differs from what they expected, they negotiate scope with the Product Owner without affecting the Sprint Goal.
  • R58. Each Increment is additive to prior Increments and must be usable. Several Increments may be created in one Sprint.

Exam trap

Swapping the three commitments is a standard wrong answer. The Product Goal is not the Sprint's commitment, and the Definition of Done is not a goal statement. Scope selected in Planning is a forecast. The commitment beside it is the Sprint Goal.

Not this

There is no fourth artifact. A burn-down is not an artifact. A release plan is not a commitment the Guide defines.

Topic 6 of 12

Done

Guide sense

The Definition of Done is a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product. It creates transparency by giving everyone the same understanding of what was completed. The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born.

Entities

  • Definition of Done — commitment of the Increment. A quality bar for the product, not a goal for a single Sprint.
  • Increment — exists only where the work meets that bar. It must be usable.
  • Developers — required to conform to the Definition of Done. They instill quality by adhering to it.
  • Sprint Retrospective — the event that inspects how the last Sprint went with regard to the Definition of Done, among other things.

Relationships

  • R60. Meeting the Definition of Done is what births an Increment.
  • R59. If an item does not meet the Definition of Done, it cannot be released and it cannot be presented at the Sprint Review. It returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration.
  • R45. An Increment that does meet the Definition of Done may be delivered before the Sprint ends. The Sprint Review is not a release gate.
  • R21. Developers are required to conform to the Definition of Done.
  • R34. During the Sprint, quality does not decrease. The bar is not lowered to protect a forecast.
  • R61. If the organization has a standard, every Scrum Team follows it as a minimum. If it does not, the Scrum Team creates a Definition of Done appropriate for the product.
  • R62. Multiple Scrum Teams on one product must mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done.
  • R47 and R48. The Retrospective inspects the Definition of Done. Improvements that raise effectiveness are addressed as soon as possible and may be added to the next Sprint Backlog.

Exam trap

"Ready" is not "Done." An item ready for selection is transparent enough that the team believes it can be Done in one Sprint. The Guide has no Definition of Ready. Lowering the Definition of Done mid-Sprint so the forecast can be met contradicts "quality does not decrease."

Not this

Done is not a sign-off by the Product Owner, a test-team exit, or a document that each team on the same product may define differently.

Topic 7 of 12

Self-Managing Teams

Guide sense

Self-managing means the Scrum Team internally decides who does what, when, and how. The Developers carry that into the Sprint: they create the plan, adapt it each day toward the Sprint Goal, and hold each other accountable as professionals. No one else tells them how to turn Product Backlog items into Increments of value.

Entities

  • Self-managing — an internal decision about who, what, when, and how. The 2020 term. "Self-organizing" is older wording.
  • Developers — accountable for the Sprint Backlog, daily adaptation, the Definition of Done, and professional accountability to each other.
  • Sprint Backlog — a plan by and for the Developers, updated as more is learned.
  • Adaptation — expected the moment inspection teaches the team something new. It is harder when the team is not self-managing.

Relationships

  • R11. Self-managing is how the Scrum Team is structured, not a privilege the Scrum Master grants.
  • R20 and R56. Creating the Sprint plan is a Developer accountability. The Sprint Backlog is theirs to keep current.
  • R22 and R43. They adapt the plan each day toward the Sprint Goal, and the Daily Scrum is not the only time they may do it.
  • R23. They hold each other accountable. That is not assignment by the Scrum Master or the Product Owner.
  • R26. How selected items become an Increment is at the sole discretion of the Developers.
  • R34 and R57. They may renegotiate scope with the Product Owner when they learn more. They do not endanger the Sprint Goal, and they do not lower quality.
  • R03 and R04. A team that is not self-managing struggles to adapt, and a Scrum Team is expected to adapt as soon as inspection gives it new information.

Exam trap

The Product Owner decides what is ordered in the Product Backlog and discusses the Sprint selection with the Developers. The Product Owner does not assign tasks, and the Scrum Master does not become the dispatcher when the team is behind.

Not this

Self-management is not isolation from the Product Owner, and it is not freedom to drop the Definition of Done or the Sprint Goal.

Topic 8 of 12

Facilitation

Guide sense

The Scrum Master is accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness and serves the team by ensuring that all Scrum events take place and are positive, productive, and kept within the timebox. Facilitation here is that service. It is not ownership of the team's plan, and it is not a requirement that the Scrum Master chair every conversation.

Entities

  • Scrum Master — ensures the events happen and stay inside the timebox; causes removal of impediments; facilitates stakeholder collaboration as requested or needed.
  • Daily Scrum — still the Developers' event. They choose its structure.
  • Impediment — something in the way of the team's progress. The Scrum Master causes its removal.
  • Stakeholder — collaboration with stakeholders is facilitated when requested or needed, not on a cadence the Guide invents.

Relationships

  • R31. Ensuring events happen, and that they are positive, productive, and timeboxed, is a Scrum Master service to the Scrum Team.
  • R40 and R42. The Daily Scrum is 15 minutes for the Developers. The Developers pick the structure and techniques. The Scrum Master does not impose the three questions.
  • R41. The Scrum Master joins that event as a Developer only when actively working on Sprint Backlog items.
  • R30. "Causing the removal of impediments" does not mean the Scrum Master personally fixes every impediment, and it does not mean the Developers wait.
  • R29 and R65. Facilitating stakeholder collaboration is a service to the Product Owner, done as requested or needed. Removing barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams is a service to the organization.

Exam trap

A Scrum Master who cancels the Retrospective to give the team more build time, or who runs the Daily Scrum as a status report to the Product Owner, has removed an inspection rather than facilitated it.

Not this

The Guide does not make the Scrum Master the meeting chair of every event, the team's secretary, or the person who assigns the Sprint tasks.

Topic 9 of 12

Coaching

Guide sense

The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide. They do this by helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice, both within the Scrum Team and the organization. They are accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness, by enabling the team to improve its practices within the framework. Scrum Masters are true leaders who serve.

Entities

  • Scrum Master — coach of the team, the Product Owner, and the organization. Not a fourth name for "project manager."
  • Scrum — the framework as defined in the Guide. Purposefully incomplete, and immutable: parts of Scrum are not Scrum.
  • Self-managing — one of the two capabilities the Scrum Master coaches in the team members.
  • Cross-functional — the other. The team has, or acquires, the skills to create value each Sprint.

Relationships

  • R27. Establishing Scrum means helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice, in the team and in the organization.
  • R28. Effectiveness is improved inside the Scrum framework, not by adding accountabilities or events the Guide does not have.
  • R29. Service to the Scrum Team includes coaching self-management and cross-functionality, and helping the team focus on high-value Increments that meet the Definition of Done.
  • R29, continued. Service to the Product Owner includes techniques for Product Goal definition and Product Backlog management, clear Product Backlog items, empirical product planning, and stakeholder collaboration as requested or needed.
  • R29, continued. Service to the organization includes leading, training, and coaching the Scrum adoption; planning and advising implementations; helping employees and stakeholders enact empiricism for complex work.
  • R30. Causing removal of impediments is part of serving the team. Coaching does not replace that service.
  • R66. Scrum wraps existing practices or renders them unnecessary. Complementary tactics are described elsewhere. Coaching someone to adopt a practice is not the same as teaching that Scrum requires it.

Exam trap

"Servant-leader" is familiar and is not the 2020 sentence. The Guide says Scrum Masters are true leaders who serve. Coaching the team does not mean doing the Product Owner's ordering for them or managing the Developers' tasks.

Not this

Coaching is not a license to change the Scrum framework, skip an event, or install SAFe, Nexus, or a company methodology as if it were in the Scrum Guide.

Topic 10 of 12

Product Value

Guide sense

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team. How that is done may vary widely across organizations, teams, and individuals. A product is a vehicle to deliver value. An Increment must be usable in order to provide value, and it is a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal.

Entities

  • Product Owner — the accountability for maximizing value. Not a committee.
  • Product — a vehicle to deliver value, with a clear boundary, known stakeholders, and well-defined users or customers. A service, a physical product, or something more abstract.
  • Product Goal — the future state the team plans against. The long-term objective in the Product Backlog.
  • Increment — usable, additive, and verified. Value that does not meet the Definition of Done is not yet an Increment.
  • Scrum Team — the whole team is accountable for a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint. The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of that product.

Relationships

  • R15. Maximizing value is a Product Owner accountability. The Guide does not name a single technique for it.
  • R14. Creating the valuable, useful Increment each Sprint is the Scrum Team's accountability.
  • R64. Value is delivered through a product with a boundary and known users or customers. Scrum is not defined as a project.
  • R51 and R55. The Product Goal sits in the Product Backlog and describes a future state. The team fulfills or abandons that one objective before taking the next.
  • R37. In Sprint Planning, the Product Owner proposes how the product could increase its value and utility in the current Sprint. The team then defines the Sprint Goal.
  • R58 and R45. The Increment must be usable. It may be delivered to stakeholders before the end of the Sprint. The Sprint Review is not a gate to releasing value.

Exam trap

Maximizing value is not the same job as ordering a list for its own sake, and it is not the Scrum Master's accountability. A Sprint full of started work that is not usable has not produced the Increment the Guide describes.

Not this

The Guide does not require a business-value formula, a value metric, or a release train. Those may be local techniques. They are not Scrum rules.

Topic 11 of 12

Product Backlog Management

Guide sense

The Product Owner is accountable for effective Product Backlog management: developing and explicitly communicating the Product Goal; creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog items; ordering Product Backlog items; and ensuring the Product Backlog is transparent, visible, and understood. The Product Owner may do this work or delegate it. The Product Owner remains accountable.

Entities

  • Product Owner — one person. The decisions are visible in the content and ordering of the Product Backlog, and in the Increment at the Sprint Review.
  • Product Backlog — emergent, ordered, the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.
  • Product Backlog refinement — ongoing. It adds detail such as a description, order, and size. Attributes vary with the domain.
  • Developers — responsible for sizing. They select Sprint items through discussion with the Product Owner.

Relationships

  • R16. The four management responsibilities are the Product Goal, the items, the order, and transparency of the Product Backlog.
  • R17. Delegation is allowed. Accountability stays with the Product Owner.
  • R18. The Product Owner is one person, not a committee, and may represent the needs of many stakeholders.
  • R19. Anyone else who wants a change tries to convince the Product Owner.
  • R54. One Product Backlog is the single source of work. Several teams on the same product share it, and they share one Product Owner (R13).
  • R63. Refinement is an ongoing activity, not an event. An item that can be Done in one Sprint is ready for selection. The Guide does not define a Definition of Ready.
  • R25. The Developers who will do the work are responsible for the sizing. The Product Owner may help them understand trade-offs.
  • R24. In Sprint Planning, the Developers select items through discussion with the Product Owner. The Product Owner does not assign the Sprint Backlog to them.

Exam trap

"The Product Owner must write every Product Backlog item personally" and "the Developers may reorder the Product Backlog" are both off. The Product Owner may delegate the work and may not delegate the accountability. Ordering stays with the Product Owner. Sizing stays with the Developers.

Not this

Grooming is an older name for refinement. A proxy Product Owner, a second backlog for the same product, and a committee of Product Owners are not in the Guide.

Topic 12 of 12

Stakeholders & Customers

Guide sense

A product has known stakeholders and well-defined users or customers. Stakeholders are not a Scrum accountability. They collaborate with the Scrum Team, especially at the Sprint Review, and they influence the Product Backlog by convincing the Product Owner. The Scrum Team is open with stakeholders about the work and the challenges.

Entities

  • Stakeholder — someone receiving the work or otherwise holding a stake. Key stakeholders are part of the Sprint Review. There is no Stakeholder accountability on the Scrum Team.
  • Product — clear boundary, known stakeholders, well-defined users or customers.
  • Product Owner — one person who may represent the needs of many stakeholders. The organization's respect for the Product Owner's decisions is a condition of the Product Owner's success.
  • Sprint Review — where the Scrum Team presents the results of its work to key stakeholders and progress toward the Product Goal is discussed.
  • Scrum Master — facilitates stakeholder collaboration as requested or needed, and removes barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams.

Relationships

  • R64. Users or customers are part of the Guide's description of a product. They are not given an accountability, an event, or an artifact.
  • R18. One Product Owner represents many stakeholders. The Product Owner is not a committee of those stakeholders.
  • R19. Stakeholders who want the Product Backlog changed try to convince the Product Owner. They do not order it.
  • R44. At the Sprint Review, the Scrum Team and stakeholders review what was accomplished and what changed in the environment, then collaborate on what to do next. The Product Backlog may be adjusted.
  • R45. Delivery to stakeholders is not held for that event. An Increment may be released earlier. The Review is a working session, not a gate and not only a presentation.
  • R65. The Scrum Master facilitates stakeholder collaboration when requested or needed, and serves the organization by removing barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams.

Exam trap

Inviting stakeholders to the Daily Scrum so they can steer the day, or letting a customer committee replace the Product Owner, both break the accountabilities. Stakeholder collaboration has a home at the Sprint Review and through the Product Owner. The Daily Scrum remains the Developers' 15 minutes.

Not this

The Guide does not require a customer-demo script, a sign-off, a stakeholder map artifact, or a product-owner proxy who speaks for a committee.

Scrum

A lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. Purposefully incomplete. Immutable: implementing only parts of it is possible, and the result is not Scrum.

"Scrum exists only in its entirety."

Not this. a method that requires user stories, velocity, or extra events.

Knowledge comes from experience and from making decisions based on what is observed. Scrum is founded on empiricism and lean thinking.

Not this. deciding from a prediction of work that has not happened.

Reduces waste and focuses on the essentials. Paired with empiricism as a foundation of Scrum. The Guide does not define a separate lean practice set.

Not this. a requirement to adopt a particular lean toolkit.

Transparency

The emergent process and work must be visible to those performing the work and those receiving it. Important decisions are based on the perceived state of the three artifacts. Low transparency leads to decisions that diminish value and increase risk.

"Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful."

"Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is considered pointless."

Not this. inspection that never changes the plan or the product.

"A Scrum Team is expected to adapt the moment it learns anything new through inspection."

Not this. waiting for the next quarterly plan to change course.

Not this. a guarantee of scope.

Not this. a rule that the team may only discuss one Product Backlog item.

Not this. a requirement to publish a particular metric.

Scrum Team

Product Owner

Accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team, and for effective Product Backlog management. One person, not a committee. May represent many stakeholders. May delegate the work and remains accountable.

Accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, and for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. A true leader who serves the Scrum Team, the Product Owner, and the organization.

"Scrum Masters are true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organization."

Not this. the 2017 job title "servant-leader," a task assigner, or the owner of the Product Backlog.

Developers

The people in the Scrum Team committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint. Skills vary by domain. Accountable for the Sprint plan, adhering to the Definition of Done, adapting that plan each day toward the Sprint Goal, and holding each other accountable as professionals.

Not this. only programmers, or people who take tasks assigned by the Scrum Master.

Used in Scrum Team, Events, Done, Self-Managing Teams, Product Backlog Management

Cross-functional

The Scrum Team members have all the skills necessary to create value each Sprint. They share or acquire skills as needed.

Not this. a separate testing or analysis team outside the Scrum Team.

Used in Scrum Team, Coaching

Self-managing

The Scrum Team internally decides who does what, when, and how.

"They are also self-managing, meaning they internally decide who does what, when, and how."

Not this. the older word "self-organizing" as the 2020 term, or a team whose Sprint tasks are assigned from outside.

Used in Scrum Team, Self-Managing Teams, Coaching

Sprint

The heartbeat of Scrum. A fixed-length container event of one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one concludes. All other events happen inside it. Creates at least a monthly inspection of progress toward the Product Goal.

Not this. a phase that can be lengthened when the forecast slips, or a Sprint 0.

Used in Events

Sprint Planning

Initiates the Sprint by laying out the work. The whole Scrum Team collaborates. Topics are Why, What, and How. Timeboxed to a maximum of eight hours for a one-month Sprint. Usually shorter for a shorter Sprint.

Not this. a meeting owned by the Scrum Master in which Developers are told what to pull.

Used in Events

Daily Scrum

15 minutes, for the Developers, same time and place every working day. Inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapts the Sprint Backlog. Developers choose the structure and techniques.

Not this. a status meeting for the Product Owner, or a required three-question format.

Used in Events, Facilitation

Sprint Review

Inspects the outcome of the Sprint and determines future adaptations. The Scrum Team and key stakeholders discuss progress toward the Product Goal. A working session. Timeboxed to a maximum of four hours for a one-month Sprint. Second to last event of the Sprint.

"The Sprint Review should never be considered a gate to releasing value."

Not this. a sign-off demo, or the only moment an Increment may be delivered.

Used in Events, Stakeholders & Customers

Sprint Retrospective

Plans ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Inspects individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done. Concludes the Sprint. Timeboxed to a maximum of three hours for a one-month Sprint.

Not this. an optional event, or a second Sprint Review about the product increment.

Used in Events, Done

Product Backlog refinement

The ongoing activity of breaking down and further defining Product Backlog items into smaller, more precise items. Adds detail such as description, order, and size. Not a Scrum event.

Not this. a sixth event, or a Definition of Ready gate.

Used in Events, Product Backlog Management

Product Backlog

An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product. The single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team. Contains the Product Goal. Commitment: the Product Goal.

Not this. one of several backlogs for the same product, or a list the stakeholders reorder directly.

Used in Artifacts, Product Backlog Management

Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Goal (why), the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint (what), and an actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how). A plan by and for the Developers. Updated throughout the Sprint. Commitment: the Sprint Goal.

Not this. a fixed scope contract, or a plan the Product Owner edits alone.

Used in Artifacts, Self-Managing Teams

Increment

A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Additive to all prior Increments and thoroughly verified. Must be usable. Multiple Increments may be created in a Sprint. Commitment: the Definition of Done.

"Work cannot be considered part of an Increment unless it meets the Definition of Done."

Not this. unfinished work shown at the Sprint Review, or a release that waits for the Sprint Review.

Used in Artifacts, Done, Product Value

Product Goal

A future state of the product that the Scrum Team can plan against. It lives in the Product Backlog. The long-term objective. The team must fulfill or abandon one objective before taking on the next. Commitment of the Product Backlog.

Not this. another name for the Sprint Goal, or several Product Goals pursued at once.

Used in Artifacts, Product Value

Sprint Goal

The single objective for the Sprint. Created in Sprint Planning and finalized before that event ends. Commitment of the Sprint Backlog, by the Developers. Creates coherence and focus, and allows flexibility in the exact work.

Not this. a promise to complete every selected Product Backlog item.

Used in Events, Artifacts

Definition of Done

A formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product. The moment a Product Backlog item meets it, an Increment is born. Creates a shared understanding of what "done" means. Commitment of the Increment.

Not this. a per-Sprint quality bargain, or a Definition of Ready.

Used in Artifacts, Done

Product

A vehicle to deliver value. It has a clear boundary, known stakeholders, and well-defined users or customers. It could be a service, a physical product, or something more abstract.

"A product is a vehicle to deliver value."

Not this. a project, or a component team with no users.

Used in Product Value, Stakeholders & Customers

Stakeholder

People with a stake in the product, including those receiving the work. Not a fourth accountability. Key stakeholders collaborate at the Sprint Review. They change the Product Backlog by convincing the Product Owner.

Not this. a committee that orders the Product Backlog, or a required daily attendee.

Used in Stakeholders & Customers

Impediment

Something blocking the Scrum Team's progress. The Scrum Master causes its removal. The Guide does not make the Scrum Master the only person who may remove it, and it does not define impediment as an artifact.

Not this. a rule that the Scrum Master personally fixes every blocker before the Developers may act.

Used in Facilitation, Coaching

Relationships

Who is accountable for what, which event serves which purpose, and which commitment belongs to which artifact.

Theory

R01

Transparency enables Inspection

Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.

Topics: Empiricism

R02

Inspection enables Adaptation

Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is pointless.

Topics: Empiricism

R03

Adaptation is harder without Self-managing

Adaptation becomes more difficult when people are not empowered or self-managing.

Topics: Empiricism, Self-Managing Teams

R04

Scrum Team adapts on Inspection

The Scrum Team adapts the moment it learns anything new through inspection.

Topics: Empiricism, Self-Managing Teams

R05

Five events provide cadence for Inspection

Scrum provides cadence in the form of its five events.

Topics: Empiricism, Events

R06

Four events inspect and adapt inside Sprint

Four formal events for inspection and adaptation sit within the containing event, the Sprint.

Topics: Events

R07

Scrum Values bring to life Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation

When the values are embodied, the pillars come to life, building trust.

Topics: Scrum Values

R08

Forecast charts do not replace Empiricism

Burn-downs, burn-ups, and cumulative flows may be used. They do not replace empiricism. Only what has already happened may be used for forward-looking decisions.

Topics: Empiricism

Team and accountabilities

R09

Scrum Team consists of one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, Developers

No other accountabilities are defined.

Topics: Scrum Team

R10

Scrum Team has no sub-teams or hierarchies

It is a cohesive unit focused on one Product Goal.

Topics: Scrum Team

R11

Scrum Team is cross-functional and self-managing

Cross-functional means the members have all the skills needed to create value each Sprint. Self-managing means they decide who does what, when, and how.

Topics: Scrum Team, Self-Managing Teams

R12

Scrum Team is typically 10 or fewer people

Smaller teams communicate better and are more productive. Too large a team should reorganize.

Topics: Scrum Team

R13

Multiple Scrum Teams on one product share Product Goal, Product Backlog, Product Owner

They remain focused on the same product.

Topics: Scrum Team, Product Backlog Management

R14

Scrum Team is accountable for a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint

This is the whole team, not one accountability alone.

Topics: Scrum Team, Product Value

R15

Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value

How this is done may vary widely.

Topics: Scrum Team, Product Value

R16

Product Owner is accountable for Product Goal, creating and communicating items, ordering, Product Backlog transparency

These four are the Guide's list for effective Product Backlog management.

Topics: Scrum Team, Product Backlog Management

R17

Product Owner may delegate Product Backlog management work

The Product Owner remains accountable.

Topics: Product Backlog Management

R18

Product Owner is one person, not a committee

The Product Owner may represent many stakeholders.

R19

Stakeholders change the Product Backlog by convincing the Product Owner

They do not order it themselves.

Instilling quality by adhering to a Definition of Done. Developers are required to conform to it.

Developers alone decide how Product Backlog items become Increments

Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide

By helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice, in the team and the organization.

During the Sprint no changes endanger Sprint Goal

Product Owner alone may cancel Sprint

Only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

The whole Scrum Team then collaborates to define the Sprint Goal.

As long as the event focuses on the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day.
